# Romanian curriculum: proposed 1,000-seed learning path

**Status: module plan approved on 2026-11-17.** Module order, scope, and seed
budgets are approved. Next, prepare the 1,000 seeds for a separate review;
the dictionary remains the live pilot until its expansion is approved.

## Design

The goal remains useful everyday Romanian with limited study time. The sequence
starts with immediate exchanges and sentence-building tools, grows into everyday
independence, and then adds more expressive and abstract language. Frequency
and practical usefulness should guide the seed selection within these themes.

The proposal has **60 modules, 1,000 seeds, and 200 five-entry Learn batches**.
Every module count is a multiple of five, ranging from 10 to 25 entries.
The phases below are planning groups; modules remain the app's learning units.

- Mix words and useful phrases. Practical modules should include relevant verbs
  and descriptive words as well as nouns. Aim for roughly a third of all seeds
  to be nouns, rather than letting the curriculum become an object-name inventory.
- Introduce high-value function words and general verbs early. Later modules on
  comparison, reasoning, or conversation add vocabulary rather than withhold
  basic words needed in the earlier modules.
- Keep related sets together where useful, including subject pronouns and
  number blocks. Grammatical variants stay inside dictionary entries and do
  not consume extra seed slots.
- Give each seed one home module. Reuse earlier seeds freely in examples and
  later scenarios without counting or teaching them as new seeds again.
- A phrase can reuse familiar words when it is a useful communicative unit.
  Do not pad the total with individual conjugations already covered by a verb.
- Counts are design targets, not a reason to add low-value vocabulary. If actual
  seed selection exposes a weak allocation, propose moving five-entry blocks
  between modules while preserving the overall 1,000-seed target.

## Selecting the seeds after approval

This is an approved curriculum design, not a measured frequency ranking. The
subsequent [1,000-seed draft](seed-review.md) follows these module budgets and
has its frequency checks and selection method documented in
[seed-sources.md](seed-sources.md). It combines frequency evidence with
everyday usefulness, consolidates inflected forms under entries, and includes
an editorial selection of useful phrases.

Counts refer to 1,000 total unique entries, including the existing 44 pilot
seeds. The pilot's module structure is not a constraint on this redesign.
Preserve existing seed IDs when moving entries between modules so the app can
continue to associate saved progress and attempt history with those entries.

Approval sequence:

1. Review the proposed modules, sequence, and counts.
2. Expand the approved plan into `seeds.json`, checking counts, uniqueness,
   coverage, ordering, frequency balance, and retention of existing pilot IDs.
3. Review the expanded seeds before generating the full `dictionary.json`.
4. Expand and validate the dictionary, including forms, examples, accepted
   answers, and unambiguous quiz prompts; then verify the app with the full data.

Until the seed and dictionary reviews are complete, the live 44-entry
dictionary remains the app's runtime vocabulary. Approval of this module plan
authorizes preparing the seeds, not publishing the expanded dictionary.
